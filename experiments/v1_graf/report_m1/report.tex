\begin{abstract}
We learn single-trial latent dynamics from streaming macaque V1 responses to a drifting
grating with stable Variational Joint Filtering (\svjf{}): an online, single-pass
state-space learner with an \emph{unknown} readout, estimated jointly by a scale-fixed
candid covariance-free incremental PCA (CCIPCA) and a projection encoder. On a single
grating direction (\ang{225}, $N{=}74$ well-tuned units, \SI{10}{ms} bins) the filtered,
leave-one-neuron predictive likelihood meets or slightly exceeds the stimulus-locked PSTH
ceiling -- \emph{with} observations, \svjf{} reconstructs held-out neurons as well as
trial-averaging. We then ask the harder question of whether the learned dynamics
\emph{forecast}: we free-run the flow from a time $t_0$ in a held-out trial (no
observations after $t_0$), decode it to a predicted firing rate, and score that forecast
against the held-out future spikes by a Poisson-deviance skill, versus two baselines --
persistence and the stimulus-locked PSTH -- over half, one, and two grating cycles. A
hyperparameter search over latent dimension, basis size, and training length shows the
autonomous free-run beats persistence only marginally and \emph{loses to the PSTH at every
horizon}; adding capacity does not help (a ceiling, not under-fitting). Inspecting the
free-run revealed an under-regularized one-step map -- shrinkage toward the origin plus
high-frequency chaos. We add a closed-form curvature penalty on the velocity field, which
turns the jagged free-run into a smooth limit cycle (an \num{8}-fold drop in trajectory
roughness) and improves held-out generalization, but does \emph{not} close the PSTH gap.
The penalty thus cleanly separates two effects: a messy, over-fit flow (fixable) from a
genuine property of stimulus-locked data -- that an autonomous free-run cannot beat
trial-averaging. The loop runs in real time (\SI{0.3}{ms}/bin).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\svjf{} learns latent dynamics and a linear-Gaussian/Poisson readout jointly from a single
streaming pass over observations \cite{zhao2020}. The additions used here are (i) a
warm-started online readout estimated by CCIPCA at the correct (covariance) scale, (ii) a
projection encoder that feeds the recognition network a subspace projection of the
observation rather than the raw spikes, and (iii) a growing radial-basis-function (RBF)
velocity flow; all are backward-compatible and default to the original VJF.

We study one drifting-grating direction in depth and ask a deliberately strict question:
not merely whether \svjf{} reconstructs the spikes online, but whether its learned
\emph{autonomous dynamics forecast} -- whether the free-running flow, decoded, predicts the
\emph{future} spikes better than trivial baselines. We make that question the sole
model-selection criterion (\cref{sec:criterion}), search for the best model under it, and
report what the dynamics can and cannot do.

\section{Dataset and task}
We use the Graf et al.\ \cite{graf2011} anesthetized-macaque Utah-array recordings:
72 equally spaced drifting-grating directions (\ang{5} spacing) $\times$ 50 trials, each
\SI{2560}{ms} (first \SI{1280}{ms} the drifting grating, then blank). We use
\texttt{array\_5}, the highest mean-rate array (\SI{10.4}{Hz}, 148 single units), the same
array analyzed by vLGP \cite{vlgp}, binned at \SI{10}{ms}. The grating temporal frequency is
\SI{6.25}{Hz}, so the response period is \SI{160}{ms} $=16$ bins. As a fixed preprocessing
step we keep neurons whose orientation tuning (sum of two von Mises bumps) fits with
$R^2\!\ge\!0.75$, leaving $N=74$ well-tuned units, and study the strongest-response
direction (\ang{225}). Its 50 trials are split, once and seed-fixed, into 30 train / 10
validation / 10 test; the search selects on validation and the test trials are reserved for
the figures here.

\Cref{fig:data} shows the data: a single-trial raster, the trial-averaged population rate
(which oscillates at the grating's \SI{6.25}{Hz} during the stimulus and decays after
offset), and the per-neuron PSTH. The units are sharply, bimodally orientation-tuned
(\cref{fig:tuning}). This is a low-to-moderate SNR regime: a rank-$d$ log-linear-Poisson fit
to the stimulus-window PSTH gives an instantaneous Fisher-information SNR
bound\footnote{\texttt{neuroFisherSNR} (\texttt{github.com/catniplab/neurofisherSNR}): how
accurately the $d$-dim latent is estimable from the spikes of a $\lambda=\exp(Cx+b)$ model.}
of $\approx\!\SI{4.7}{dB}$ ($R^2\,0.66$) at $d{=}3$ -- comparable to the \SI{3}{dB} synthetic
regime of the original \svjf{} study. Over the stimulus window the population traces a
low-dimensional, noisy limit cycle at the grating frequency (\cref{fig:pca}); this cyclic
structure is what the autonomous flow is meant to capture.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Forward step and learning.}
Each bin maximizes one filtering-step ELBO,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \underbrace{\E_{q(x_t)}\!\log p(y_t\mid x_t)}_{\text{reconstruction}}
  + \underbrace{\E_{q(x_t)}\!\log p\big(x_t \mid \tilde x_{t-1},\theta\big)}_{\text{dynamics}}
  + \underbrace{H\big(q(x_t)\big)}_{\text{entropy}},
  \label{eq:elbo}
\end{equation}
where $q_t=\mathcal N(\mu_t,\Sigma_t)$ is the recognition posterior, $\tilde x_t$ a
reparameterized sample, $p(y_t\mid x_t)$ a Poisson likelihood with rate
$\lambda_t=\exp(Cx_t+b)$, and the dynamics term is Gaussian about the RBF velocity flow
$x_t=\tilde x_{t-1}+f(\tilde x_{t-1})$. Parameters are learned by a hybrid per-step scheme:
a stochastic-gradient (Adam) step on \cref{eq:elbo} (recognition and the flow weights) plus
closed-form non-gradient updates of the likelihood noise. The latent is reset to the prior
at each trial onset (per-trial reset).

\paragraph{Unknown readout (scale-fixed CCIPCA + projection encoder).}
The loading $(C,b)$ is estimated online by CCIPCA on a causal, link-matched feature
$\tilde g(y)=\log(\nu_t+c)$, where $\nu_t$ is a leaky integrator of the counts and the
running mean of $\tilde g$ is $b$. CCIPCA maintains $m$ vectors $\{v_i\}$ converging to
$\lambda_i e_i$ (eigenvalue $\times$ eigenvector of $\Sigma_g=\E[(\tilde g-b)(\tilde
g-b)^\trp]$); the loading column $c_i=\hat v_i\sqrt{\norm{v_i}}=e_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ whitens
the recognition input, $\cov(\Renc)=\Lambda^{-1/2}U^\trp\Sigma_g U\Lambda^{-1/2}=\identity$.
The warm start must seed $\{v_i\}$ at the same \emph{covariance} scale ($\norm{v_i}=\lambda_i$)
the streaming update converges to; seeding at \emph{scatter} scale ($\norm{v_i}\approx
T\lambda_i$) makes the loading shrink by $\sim\!\sqrt T$ over training and the latent inflate.
Dividing the warm-start scatter by the sample count pins the loading and $\cov(\Renc)$ across
all of training (\cref{fig:scalefix}). The recognition reads $\Renc=C^{+}(\tilde g(y)-b)$;
$(C,b)$ is Procrustes-anchored into the decoder every $K$ steps.

\paragraph{Growing RBF flow and a curvature regularizer.}
\label{sec:curv}
The velocity is $f(x)=\sum_j \phi_j(x)\,W_{j,:}$ with Gaussian RBFs
$\phi_j(x)=\exp(-\norm{x-c_j}^2/2w_j^2)$; the basis is seeded by $k$-means on the warm-up
buffer and grown by novelty (a new center, resource-allocating weight init, where coverage
falls below threshold). Trained by gradient alone, this one-step map is under-regularized:
its free-run shrinks toward the origin with high-frequency chaos (\cref{sec:reg}). We add a
closed-form penalty on the field \emph{curvature}, which suppresses high-frequency wiggle
while leaving an affine/rotational field (a limit cycle) unpenalized. With
$\partial^2\phi_j/\partial x\partial x^\trp=\phi_j\big[(x-c_j)(x-c_j)^\trp/w_j^4 -
I/w_j^2\big]$,
\begin{equation}
  H_a(x)=\frac{\partial^2 f_a}{\partial x\,\partial x^\trp}
        =\sum_j W_{j,a}\,\phi_j(x)\Big[\tfrac{(x-c_j)(x-c_j)^\trp}{w_j^4}-\tfrac{I}{w_j^2}\Big],
  \qquad
  R_2(x)=\sum_a \norm{H_a(x)}_F^2,
  \label{eq:curv}
\end{equation}
and the gradient flow minimizes $\mathcal{L}+\lambda\,\E_t R_2(x_t)$, evaluated at the
filtered mean each step. $\lambda{=}0$ recovers the original ELBO; the reconstruction,
recognition, and closed-form updates are untouched.

\paragraph{Train/validation/test split and multi-epoch online training.}
The stream opens with a coverage warm-up (dynamics off) over which the readout is fit and
the latent means buffered; the RBF flow is then initialized once and turned on. The 30 train
trials are replayed for $E$ epochs in a randomized order, with the posterior reset at each
trial onset, so the readout and flow keep adapting while no information leaks across trials.
Each epoch is one strictly causal per-sample pass (no minibatching). Evaluation freezes the
model and runs the held-out trials.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\svjf{} on V1, one streaming pass (per-trial reset), with the scale-fixed CCIPCA
  warm-start, the growing RBF flow, and the curvature penalty. Differs from the original
  \svjf{} loop \cite{zhao2020} only in the boxed lines.}
\label{alg:svjf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require trials; warm-up trials $W$; refresh $K$; smoothing $\tau$; curvature weight $\lambda$
\State init readout state $\nu,b,\{v_i\}$; posterior $q\gets\varnothing$; buffer $\mathcal B\gets[\,]$
\Statex \textit{Warm-up (first $W$ trials, dynamics off): fit the readout, buffer latent means}
\For{each bin $y_t$ in the first $W$ trials}
  \State update $\nu,b$; CCIPCA step on $\tilde g-b$;\ \ filter $y_t$ (dynamics off); append $\mu_t$ to $\mathcal B$
\EndFor
\State \fbox{$v_i\gets \lambda_i e_i$ from the \emph{covariance} (not scatter) of $\mathcal B$}\Comment{scale fix}
\State seed RBF centers by $k$-means on $\mathcal B$; turn the dynamics on
\Statex \textit{Online (train trials $\times E$ epochs, randomized; reset $q$ each trial onset)}
\For{each bin $y_t$}
  \State $\mu_t,q\gets\textsc{VjfFilter}(y_t,\,C^{+}(\tilde g-b),\,q)$ \Comment{recognition + flow}
  \State \fbox{gradient step on $\mathcal L+\lambda\,R_2(\mu_t)$}\Comment{curvature penalty, \cref{eq:curv}}
  \State \fbox{\textbf{if} novelty$(\tilde x_t)<\theta_{\mathrm{grow}}$: add an RBF center (RAN weight init)}\Comment{growing basis}
  \If{$t\bmod K=0$} Procrustes-refresh $(C,b)$ into the decoder \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{The forecast-reconstruction criterion.}
\label{sec:criterion}
The sole model-selection metric is \emph{future forecasted reconstruction}. From many start
phases $t_0$ in a held-out trial we free-run the flow with no observations after $t_0$,
decode it to a rate, and score the forecast against the future spike counts by the Poisson
deviance $D=2\sum[n\log(n/\lambda)-(n-\lambda)]$ over $k=8,16,32$ bins (half, one, two
grating cycles). We report the skill $\mathrm{skill}_k=1-D_{\mathrm{model}}(k)/D_{\mathrm{base}}(k)$
relative to two baselines: \emph{persistence} (hold the model's $t_0$ rate) and the
stimulus-locked \emph{PSTH} (the train trial-average at each absolute bin); the pre-declared
selection score is $S=0.5\,\mathrm{skill}_8+0.3\,\mathrm{skill}_{16}+0.2\,\mathrm{skill}_{32}$
versus persistence, gated by a decent filtered reconstruction. Scoring the decoded rate
against the observed future spikes -- not a latent-space $R^2$ -- removes the affine-overfit
loophole of self-referential latent metrics. We additionally report the filtered
\emph{leave-one-neuron} predictive log-likelihood (PLL, bits/spike; \cite{vlgp}) against the
PSTH-ceiling PLL, and per-bin wall-clock.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Reconstruction reaches the PSTH ceiling.}
With observations, \svjf{} reconstructs held-out neurons as well as trial-averaging: the
filtered leave-one-neuron PLL on the held-out trials is \num{0.67}--\num{0.72} bits/spike,
at or slightly above the stimulus-locked PSTH ceiling (\num{0.64}--\num{0.68}, depending on
the split). Reconstruction is therefore not the bottleneck, and every searched configuration
clears the reconstruction gate. The question is whether the \emph{dynamics} add anything
beyond this.

\paragraph{The autonomous free-run beats persistence but not the PSTH; capacity does not
help.} Selecting on the forecast-reconstruction score over a search of latent dimension
($L\in\{3,4,5,6\}$), RBF budget (up to \num{3200} centers, growing as $\sim\!2^L$), training
length ($E\in\{20,\dots,200\}$), learning rate and denoising, the best free-run beats
persistence only marginally ($S\approx{+}0.03$: it reduces the forecast Poisson deviance by
$\sim\!3\%$ relative to holding the $t_0$ rate) and \emph{loses to the stimulus-locked PSTH
at every horizon} ($\mathrm{skill}_k$ vs.\ PSTH $\approx{-}0.03$ to ${-}0.04$;
\cref{fig:reg}B). Crucially, this is a ceiling and not under-fitting: raising the latent
dimension to $L{=}6$, the basis to thousands of centers, or training to 200 epochs does
\emph{not} improve the forecast (the largest models score near zero), and the per-horizon
profile is flat across half, one, and two cycles. For a stimulus-locked grating the PSTH is a
phase-aware, near-oracle predictor of a held-out trial -- it ``knows'' the rate at every
future bin -- whereas the autonomous flow must propagate the phase from $t_0$ through its
learned field and accumulates error. That \emph{filtered} reconstruction matches the PSTH
while the \emph{forecast} does not precisely measures the forecastable structure the
autonomous dynamics fail to carry.

\paragraph{A curvature penalty turns a jagged flow into a smooth cycle.}
\label{sec:reg}
Inspecting the best free-run exposed an under-regularized one-step map: the autonomous
trajectory shrinks toward the origin while jittering at high frequency (the gradient-trained
flow with a large basis memorizes per-bin spike noise into the velocity field). The
curvature penalty of \cref{eq:curv} fixes this directly. As $\lambda$ increases, the velocity
field's mean curvature falls two orders of magnitude and the free-run trajectory roughness
(mean second difference) drops $\sim$\num{8}-fold, while the forecast skill holds or slightly
rises through $\lambda\!\sim\!10^{-3}$ before over-regularization flattens the field toward
persistence (\cref{fig:reg}A); $\lambda\!\gtrsim\!1$ destabilizes the gradient step. At the
selected $\lambda{=}10^{-3}$ the held-out free-run is a smooth trajectory that tracks the
trial-averaged cycle for the first part of the horizon and then relaxes inward
(\cref{fig:freerun}), in contrast to the jagged collapse without the penalty. The penalty
also improves test-set generalization (the held-out forecast skill rises relative to the
unregularized model). It does \emph{not}, however, close the PSTH gap: the smoothed free-run
still loses to trial-averaging. The penalty thus disentangles two distinct issues -- a messy,
over-fit flow (which it fixes, yielding interpretable dynamics) from a genuine property of
stimulus-locked data, that an autonomous free-run cannot beat the trial average.

\paragraph{Real-time.}
The per-bin cost of the online learning step is \SI{0.3}{ms} (frozen inference
\SI{0.1}{ms}), measured on an M-series CPU at \num{1600} RBF centers -- about \num{30}$\times$
inside the \SI{10}{ms} bin, so the basis size is not a real-time constraint. The per-timestep
loop is CPU-bound; a GPU does not help.

\section{Discussion}
On a single grating direction, \svjf{} learns online -- with an unknown readout -- to
reconstruct held-out V1 neurons at the stimulus-locked PSTH ceiling. Its autonomous
dynamics, however, do not \emph{forecast} the future spikes better than that trial average:
under a strict forecasted-reconstruction criterion the free-run beats only the weak
persistence baseline and loses to the PSTH, and adding latent dimension, basis size, or
training length does not change this. We trace the visibly pathological free-run -- shrinkage
plus high-frequency chaos -- to an under-regularized one-step map, and a closed-form
curvature penalty restores a smooth limit-cycle free-run that generalizes better; but the
smoothed flow still does not beat trial-averaging. The clean reading is that for
stimulus-locked grating data the PSTH is a near-oracle and the achievable single-trial
\emph{forecast} gain is small, separate from whether the flow is well-behaved.

\paragraph{Limitations and next steps.} The criterion is the strictest reasonable one for an
autonomous model and may understate the value of \svjf{} for tasks with genuine trial-to-trial
dynamics rather than a repeated stimulus drive. The most promising structural change -- out of
scope here -- is to give the flow the stimulus as an input, so direction- and
phase-specific dynamics need not be reproduced by a free-running autonomous cycle; this also
sets up the multi-direction case, which we study separately.
