\title{Stabilizing online variational joint filtering on macaque V1:\\
a readout scale fix, flow stability, and the multi-condition encoder}
\author{CatNip Lab}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We apply stable Variational Joint Filtering (\svjf{}) -- an online, single-pass
variational state-space learner with an \emph{unknown} readout -- to a streaming
macaque V1 dataset (Graf et al.\ 2011) of drifting gratings. A first end-to-end run on
all 72 grating directions failed: the leave-one-neuron predictive likelihood fell below a
homogeneous-Poisson baseline and single-trial orientation decoding sat at chance, a
\emph{multi-condition encoder collapse}. Diagnosing this exposed a separate, quantitative
bug in the online readout: the CCIPCA warm-start seeded its principal vectors at
\emph{scatter} scale ($\sim\!T\lambda$) while the streaming update converges to
\emph{covariance} scale ($\lambda$), so the loading shrank by $\sim\!\sqrt{T}$ over
training and the latent inflated. A one-line fix (divide the warm-start scatter by the
sample count) pins the encoder-input variance and, on a single direction, yields a
predictive likelihood at $\sim\!90\%$ of the stimulus-locked PSTH ceiling with a live
free-run forecast. We then show that the closed-form square-root RLS flow over-fits the
filtered transitions and produces a \emph{divergent} free-run within a few replay epochs,
whereas a gradient-trained (SGD/Adam) flow stays stable; that the learned cycle is not a
genuine attractor (pointwise Jacobian spectral radius $>1$ everywhere), which makes the
free-run fragile; and that injecting a small, fit-gated, decaying-raised-sinusoid noise
into the dynamics term restores a stable forecast across all of training. Finally,
re-running the full multi-direction task with the scale fix substantially rescues the
encoder collapse -- orientation decoding reaches $\sim\!14\times$ chance at 72 directions
-- but the flow-stabilization machinery that helps single directions \emph{trades off}
against condition separation. We conclude that latent-scale control is the recurring
lever for \svjf{} on real, low-rate Poisson data, and that the multi-condition encoder is
only partially solved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\svjf{} learns latent dynamics and a linear-Gaussian/Poisson readout jointly from a single
streaming pass over observations \cite{zhao2020}. The ``stable'' additions relevant here
are (i) a square-root (Potter) RLS dynamics learner, (ii) a warm-started, two-timescale
online readout estimated by candid covariance-free incremental PCA (CCIPCA), and (iii) a
projection encoder that feeds the recognition network a subspace projection of the
observation rather than the raw spikes. All three are backward-compatible; the defaults
reproduce the original VJF.

The question we ask is whether \svjf{} can, online and with an unknown readout, recover
interpretable single-trial dynamics from \emph{real} macaque V1 responses to drifting
gratings -- and, in particular, whether one shared autonomous latent flow plus a learned
readout can separate the 72 stimulus conditions. This report documents (a) the failure of
the first full-task run and its diagnosis, (b) a quantitative readout bug uncovered in the
process and its fix, (c) the dynamics-stability work needed to obtain a usable single-trial
forecast, and (d) what does and does not transfer to the multi-condition setting.

\section{Dataset and task}
We use the Graf et al.\ \cite{graf2011} anesthetized-macaque Utah-array recordings:
72 equally spaced drifting-grating directions ($5^\circ$ spacing) $\times$ 50 trials
$=3600$ trials, each \SI{2560}{ms} (first \SI{1280}{ms} the drifting grating, then blank).
We use \texttt{array\_5}, the highest mean-rate array (\SI{10.4}{Hz}, 148 single units), the
same array analyzed by vLGP \cite{vlgp}, binned at \SI{10}{ms}. The grating temporal frequency is
\SI{6.25}{Hz}, so the response period is \SI{160}{ms} $=16$ bins. We keep neurons whose
orientation tuning (sum of two von Mises bumps) fits with $R^2\!\ge\!0.75$, leaving $N=74$
well-tuned units, and split 40 train / 10 test trials per direction.

\section{\svjf{} on streaming V1}
\paragraph{Forward step and learning.}
Each bin runs one ELBO filtering step: the recognition network encodes $y_t$ into a
Gaussian posterior $q_t$; the RBF flow predicts a prior $p_t$ from the previous state; the
affine Poisson decoder maps $q_t$ to a reconstruction $\lambda_t=\exp(C x_t + b)$.
Parameters are learned by a hybrid scheme per step: an SGD step on the ELBO plus
closed-form non-gradient updates of the likelihood noise and (for the RLS learners) the
flow weights. The latent state is reset to the prior at each trial onset (per-trial
reset); orientation is therefore not in the initial state and must be injected each bin by
the encoder.

\paragraph{Unknown readout (projection encoder).}
The loading $(C,b)$ is estimated online by CCIPCA on a causal, link-matched feature
$\tilde g(y)=\log(\nu_t + c)$, where $\nu_t$ is a leaky integrator of the counts. The
recognition reads the subspace projection $\Renc = C^{+}(\tilde g(y)-b)$ rather than the
raw spikes; $(C,b)$ is Procrustes-anchored into the decoder every $K$ steps. CCIPCA is
decoupled from the ELBO gradient, which collapses under sparse low-rate Poisson.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\emph{Leave-one-neuron-out predictive log-likelihood} (PLL, bits/spike; \cite{vlgp}, Eq.~39):
for each held-out neuron $n$, filter the trial from the \emph{other} neurons' projection
and score $\lambda_{:,n}$ against counts, relative to a homogeneous-Poisson baseline
$\bar y$; $0$ equals the mean-rate baseline. \emph{Orientation decode accuracy}:
cross-validated multinomial logistic regression of the 72-way label from the per-trial
latent summary (chance $1/72=0.014$). \emph{Free-run forecast $R^2$}: affine-aligned $R^2$
of a $k$-step free run of the learned flow against the inferred latent path. The
\emph{PSTH ceiling} is the leave-one-neuron PLL of a stimulus-locked trial-averaged
predictor.

\section{The first run failed: a multi-condition encoder collapse}
The full 72-direction run (L=3, $N=74$, one shared autonomous flow) was a clean NO-GO:
PLL $=-3.50$ bits/spike (worse than baseline), single-trial decode $=0.014$ (chance), even
though the autonomous flow learned a cycle (forecast $R^2=0.31$) and the loop ran in real
time (\SI{2.72}{ms}/bin, zero divergences). A decode cascade localized where orientation is
lost: it is strongly present in the raw counts ($0.34$) and the log-rate feature ($0.30$),
already reduced by the best rank-3 subspace ($0.23$), halved again at the encoder
\emph{input} $\Renc$ ($0.13$; the online CCIPCA subspace sat $46.5^\circ$ off the ideal
PCA-3 subspace), and finally collapses to chance at the recognition \emph{output}. A
no-dynamics control decoded no better, exonerating the flow: the failure is a Poisson
\emph{encoder} collapse, compounded by a suboptimal readout subspace.

\section{A quantitative readout bug: the CCIPCA scale fix}
\label{sec:scalefix}
Diagnosing the readout exposed a scale inconsistency in the online estimator. The streaming
CCIPCA update is a running mean whose fixed point is $\|v_i\| \to \lambda_i$, the variance
along principal component $i$; with $C=U\Lambda^{1/2}$ the pseudo-inverse whitens the
encoder input,
\begin{equation}
\cov(\Renc) = \Lambda^{-1/2}U^\trp\,\Sigma_g\,U\Lambda^{-1/2} = \identity,
\qquad \Sigma_g = U\Lambda U^\trp .
\end{equation}
But the warm start seeded the vectors from the \emph{scatter} matrix
($\|v_i\| \approx T\lambda_i$, $T$ the warm-up window), a factor $T$ too large. Over
training $\|v\|$ migrates from $T\lambda$ down toward $\lambda$; since the number of stream
steps is $\ll T^2$, it never arrives. As $\|v\|$ shrinks, $C$ shrinks ($\propto\sqrt{\|v\|}$),
$C^{+}$ grows, and the latent inflates to keep $Cx$ matching the data: a latent-scale
inflation in which the running spread grows $0.07\to0.35$ over 50 epochs and the
nearest-RBF-center distance grows $\sim\!27\times$. The fix is one line:
divide the warm-start scatter by the sample count so the seed matches the streaming fixed
point. On stationary data the loading norm and $\cov(\Renc)$ are then pinned across all of
training (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalefix}); with the bug they drift by an order of magnitude.

\section{Single-direction dynamics: flow stability and denoising}
With the scale fix in place we validate the machinery on a single direction
($225^\circ$, strongest response), replaying its 40 train trials over $E$ epochs with
per-trial reset and evaluating leave-one-neuron PLL and the free-run forecast on 10
held-out trials. PLL reaches $\sim\!0.66$ bits/spike, about $90\%$ of the PSTH ceiling
($0.73$); the model captures stimulus-locked structure though it does not yet beat the
trial-averaged predictor.

\paragraph{The RLS flow over-fits into a divergent free-run.}
The square-root RLS flow attains an excellent forecast within one epoch
($R^2\!\approx\!0.8$) but, under replay, over-solves the (observation-corrected) filtered
transitions and the autonomous free-run \emph{diverges permanently} by epoch 8
(Fig.~\ref{fig:flow}, blue). A gradient-trained (SGD/Adam) flow does not wreck the
warm-started field this way: its free-run stays alive across all 50 epochs. We therefore
adopt the SGD/Adam flow for this regime. We further give the growing basis a non-zero,
resource-allocating weight init (the new center takes the current flow error at its
location), since SGD cannot fill a zero-weight grown center fast enough.

\paragraph{The cycle is not an attractor, so the free-run is fragile.}
Instrumenting the gradient flow shows the forecast does not converge: it peaks early
($\sim\!0.7$), collapses mid-training, and partially recovers. None of the gross signals
(velocity magnitude, weight norm, latent spread) changes at the collapse; the diagnostic
is the flow-map Jacobian spectral radius, which is $>1$ \emph{everywhere}
(1.0--1.5, peaks up to 5), including when the forecast is good. The learned ``cycle'' is
locally expansive and has no stable attracting orbit; the free-run tracks the data only
while the field transiently aligns, so small gradient-driven changes flip it between
tracking and wandering off (Fig.~\ref{fig:flow}, middle).

\paragraph{Denoising noise restores a stable forecast.}
We inject a small noise into the latent that feeds the dynamics term only -- training the
flow to map off-cycle states back onto the trajectory -- on a decaying-raised-sinusoid
schedule $\sigma(t)=\sigma_0\,\gamma^{t/P}\cdot\tfrac12(1-\cos 2\pi t/P)$ (period
$P=1000$ steps), gated by the flow's fit so a still-underfitting flow is not perturbed.
Every noise amplitude we tried eliminates the collapse: the free-run holds $R^2\approx
0.5$--$0.6$ across all of training, versus the no-noise trench (Fig.~\ref{fig:flow},
right). The injection is default-off and leaves the baseline ELBO unchanged.

\section{Multi-direction: the encoder rescue and a stabilization trade-off}
Re-running the full multi-condition task (arms differing only in the readout/flow stack;
one shared autonomous flow, per-trial reset) shows that the scale fix substantially
rescues the original encoder collapse. With the scale-fixed original flow
(\texttt{srrls\_base}), orientation decoding is well above chance and its chance-ratio
\emph{grows} with the number of directions, reaching $13.8\times$ chance at 72 directions
(decode $0.19$ vs chance $0.014$); the encoder no longer collapses (Table~\ref{tab:multidir},
Fig.~\ref{fig:multidir}).

The dynamics-stabilization machinery that was essential for the single direction does not
overturn this. We compare \texttt{srrls\_base} against the previous full stack
(\texttt{sgd\_grow\_noise}: SGD growing flow $+$ denoising noise) and a refined stack
(\texttt{sgd\_grow\_refined}: a larger, smaller-width basis with harder-annealed, fit-gated
noise). The refinements help the dynamics arm -- they fill the basis as intended (the grown
basis reaches its full $400$ centers at 8--24 directions, versus the previous run, which
under-grew to $78$--$118$), and they raise decode from $3.5\times$ to $4.6\times$ chance
and PLL from $0.857$ to $0.869$ -- but \texttt{srrls\_base} remains the strongest arm for
both condition separation ($8.0\times$ chance) and reconstruction (PLL $0.903$). The
dynamics arms win only the (single-trial, noisy) forecast. The trade-off therefore
persists across the refinement: a single shared autonomous flow asked both to stabilize a
cycle and to preserve the direction information the encoder injects does the former at the
expense of the latter. \emph{The scale fix is the multi-condition lever; the dynamics
stabilization is a single-direction tool that competes with condition separation here.}

\section{Discussion}
The recurring lever across both regimes is \emph{latent-scale control}. In the single
direction the readout refresh inflates the latent; the root cause was a CCIPCA warm-start
that seeded the wrong scale, and fixing it pins the encoder input. In the full task the
encoder collapses, and the same scale fix substantially recovers orientation information.
The dynamics, by contrast, are a secondary and double-edged factor: the RLS flow
over-fits into divergence and even the gradient flow learns a non-attracting cycle, which
a small fit-gated denoising noise stabilizes -- but that stabilization trades off against
multi-condition separation, suggesting one shared autonomous flow is the wrong inductive
bias for many conditions.

\paragraph{Limitations and next steps.} The single-direction PLL sits just below the PSTH
ceiling: \svjf{} captures stimulus-locked structure but does not yet add single-trial
predictive power, which likely needs encoder/readout work (the online subspace is still
$\sim\!46^\circ$ off the ideal). Multi-condition decoding, while well above chance, is
modest in absolute terms. The single-trial forecast metric is noisy. The most promising
structural change -- deliberately out of scope here -- is to give the flow the stimulus as
an input so direction-specific dynamics need not compete with a shared cycle.
