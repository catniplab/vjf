\begin{center}\large\textbf{Projection-input recognition and decoupled readout estimation}\end{center}

\paragraph{Model recap (unchanged).}
With latent $\vx_t\in\R^m$, observation $\vy_t\in\R^n$, input $\vu_t$, the generative
model is the point-process / GLM observation and additive RBF dynamics of the paper,
\begin{align}
  \vy_t &\sim P\big(g(\vC\vx_t+\vb)\big), \qquad g=\exp \tag{lik}\\
  \vx_{t+1} &= \vx_t + f(\vx_t) + \bm{B}_t\vu_t + \bm{\epsilon}_{t+1},\quad
  f(\vx)=\vW\vphi(\vx),\quad \bm{\epsilon}_t\sim\mathcal N(\bm0,\sigma^2\bm I), \tag{gen}
\end{align}
and the recursive amortized posterior $q(\vx_t)=\mathcal N(\vmu_t,\vs_t)$ is produced by a
recognition MLP $h$, optimizing the reparameterized filtering ELBO
\begin{align}
  [\vmu_t,\vs_t] &= h(\vy_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1}), \tag{recog}\\
  \hat{\mathcal L} &= \log p(\vy_t\mid\tilde\vx_t,\theta)
     + \E_{q(\vx_t)}\log p(\vx_t\mid\tilde\vx_{t-1},\theta) + H(q(\vx_t)). \tag{ELBO}
\end{align}
The paper notes (lik) is identifiable only up to an invertible $\vR$:
$\vC\vx_t=(\vC\vR)(\vR^{-1}\vx_t)$; it initializes $\vC$ by factor analysis and
\emph{column-wise $\ell_2$-normalizes} $\vC$ every iteration, which fixes the column scale (the
diagonal of $\vR$) but leaves the rotation/reflection free.

\paragraph{The problem.}
Estimating $\vC$ by $\partial\hat{\mathcal L}/\partial\vC$ under sparse, low-rate Poisson
collapses to the trivial ``mean rate via $\vb$'' solution: the gradient on $\vC$ is scaled
by the rate $g(\vC\vx_t+\vb)\!\approx\!10^{-1}$/bin, so $\vC$ barely moves and a basin with
$\vC\!\to\!0$ absorbs the loss. Separately, $h(\vy_t,\dots)$ must invert $n$ sparse channels,
an $n\!\to\!m$ map that is hard to learn at low SNR.

\paragraph{Update (two coupled changes; the ELBO and generative model are unchanged).}
\textbf{(1) Recognition input.} Replace the raw observation $\vy_t$ in (recog) by a
deterministic, $\vC$-dependent feature --- the least-squares projection of a link-matched
observation feature onto the current loading subspace:
\begin{align}
  \vpi_t &= \vC^{+}\,(\tilde g(\vy_t)-\vb), \qquad \vC^{+}=(\vC^\top\vC)^{-1}\vC^\top\in\R^{m\times n},
     &&\text{(subspace projection)}\\
  [\vmu_t,\vs_t] &= h(\vpi_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1}), &&\text{(recog$'$)}
\end{align}
where $\tilde g(\cdot)$ is an observation-model-specific, variance-stabilizing feature
(an approximate inverse link) and $\vb$ is the running mean of $\tilde g(\vy_t)$. For the
point-process/Poisson case $\tilde g(\vy_t)=\log(\vnu_t+c)$ with a causally-smoothed count
$\vnu_t=(1-\alpha)\vnu_{t-1}+\alpha\vy_t$ ($\alpha=1/\tau$); for Gaussian observations
$\tilde g=$ identity.
$\vpi_t$ is the readout's own approximate inverse, so $h$ need only perform an $m\!\to\!m$
correction rather than invert $n$ channels. Crucially the recognition input shrinks from $\R^n$
to $\R^m$ with $m\!\ll\!n$ (here $2$ vs.\ $50$--$250$): the MLP's input layer no longer scales
with the population and never sees the raw, high-dimensional, sparse spike vector --- only its
$m$-dimensional subspace coordinate. Because $q(\vx_t)$ may be \emph{any}
data-dependent distribution, parameterizing it through $\vpi_t$ leaves the bound
$\hat{\mathcal L}$ valid (same likelihood, dynamics, entropy); only the amortization family
changes. $\vC^{+}$ is recomputed only when $\vC$ is refreshed (below) and reused as one
$O(nm)$ map per step --- not re-solved per bin. Any feature timescale $\tau$ is a fixed
hyperparameter ($\tau\ll$ the dynamics timescale), \emph{not} ELBO-learned: it affects only
the recognition input ($h$ also sees $\vmu_{t-1}$ to compensate the lag), is differentiable
in $\alpha$, and could be learned, but we set or sweep it.

\textbf{(2) Decoupled online readout estimation.} Replace $\partial\hat{\mathcal L}/\partial\vC$
by an online incremental-PCA estimate $\hat\vC_t$ of the centered smoothed log-rate: track
the top-$m$ subspace of $\vl_t-\bar\vb_t$ (e.g. CCIPCA/Oja), scaled so the implied latent has
unit variance, with $\vb=\bar\vb_t$ the running mean of $\vl_t$. The \emph{same} $\hat\vC_t$
appears in the decoder (lik) and in the projection $\vpi_t$. This is an online M-step
(second-moment estimator), well-conditioned and free of the rate-scaled-gradient collapse.
$\hat\vC_t$ is refreshed every $K$ steps.

\textbf{(3) Identifiability / normalization.} The paper's column-$\ell_2$ normalization fixes
only the \emph{scale} gauge (diagonal of $\vR$). Our setting needs more: each incremental-PCA
refresh returns a subspace basis with an \emph{arbitrary} rotation and sign (eigenvectors are
signed, near-degenerate eigenvalues rotate freely), so we fix the full orthogonal gauge by
aligning $\hat\vC_t$ to the previous estimate with orthogonal Procrustes (below), and fix the
scale by the unit-variance column scaling of (2), $\vc_i=\ve_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}$. This pins the
latent coordinate frame across refreshes so the dynamics $f=\vW\vphi$ stay valid. For a pure
rotation $\vR^\star$ the RBF flow is re-expressed exactly by rotating the centers
$\vc_i\mapsto\vR^\star\vc_i$ (and $\vW$ accordingly).

\paragraph{Estimator details (feature, CCIPCA, Procrustes).}
\emph{Link-matched feature (causal exponential smoothing).} Both the recognition input and the
incremental PCA consume the variance-stabilized feature $\vl_t=\tilde g(\vy_t)$, an exponential
moving average of the counts followed by the inverse link:
\begin{align}
  \vnu_t = (1-\alpha)\,\vnu_{t-1} + \alpha\,\vy_t,\qquad
  \tilde g(\vy_t) = \log(\vnu_t + c)\ \ (\text{Poisson}),\quad \tilde g=\mathrm{id}\ \ (\text{Gaussian}),
  \tag{EMA}
\end{align}
with smoothing rate $\alpha=1/\tau$ (memory ${\sim}\tau$ bins); $\tau{=}1$ gives $\vnu_t=\vy_t$,
i.e.\ no smoothing. The running mean $\bar\vb_t$ of $\vl_t$ supplies the bias $\vb$.

\emph{Incremental PCA (CCIPCA; Weng et al.\ 2003).} For the centered feature
$\vu_t=\vl_t-\bar\vb_t$ we maintain $m$ vectors $\vv_1,\dots,\vv_m$, each converging to
$\lambda_i\ve_i$ (eigenvalue $\times$ eigenvector of the feature covariance
$\E[\vu\vu^\top]$) without ever forming that $n\times n$ matrix. With $\vu_t^{(1)}=\vu_t$ and
rate $\eta_t=1/t$, for $i=1,\dots,m$:
\begin{align}
  \vv_i &\leftarrow (1-\eta_t)\,\vv_i + \eta_t\,\big(\vu_t^{(i)\top}\hat\vv_i\big)\,\vu_t^{(i)},
     \qquad \hat\vv_i=\vv_i/\lVert\vv_i\rVert, &&\text{(Hebb / Oja)}\\
  \vu_t^{(i+1)} &= \vu_t^{(i)} - \big(\vu_t^{(i)\top}\hat\vv_i\big)\,\hat\vv_i.
     &&\text{(deflate)}
\end{align}
Each component takes a Hebbian step toward the projection of the current residual onto its own
direction --- an online power iteration on the covariance, since
$\E[(\vu^\top\hat\vv_i)\vu]=\E[\vu\vu^\top]\hat\vv_i$ --- after which Gram--Schmidt deflation
removes that direction so component $i{+}1$ sees only the orthogonal complement. At convergence
$\lVert\vv_i\rVert=\lambda_i$ and $\hat\vv_i=\ve_i$, so the loading column
$\vc_i=\hat\vv_i\sqrt{\lVert\vv_i\rVert}=\ve_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ gives a unit-variance latent.

\emph{Orthogonal Procrustes anchoring.} A refreshed loading $\hat\vC$ is determined only up to
$\vR\in O(m)$. To pin the latent frame to the previous estimate $\vC_{\mathrm{prev}}$ we solve
\begin{equation}
  \vR^\star=\arg\min_{\vR^\top\vR=\vI}\lVert\hat\vC\vR-\vC_{\mathrm{prev}}\rVert_F=\vU\vV^\top,
  \qquad \hat\vC^\top\vC_{\mathrm{prev}}=\vU\vSig\vV^\top\ (\text{SVD}),
\end{equation}
and adopt $\hat\vC\vR^\star$. This fixes the $\vC\vx=(\vC\vR)(\vR^{-1}\vx)$ gauge at each refresh
so the dynamics $f=\vW\vphi$ stay in a consistent coordinate system.

\paragraph{Special cases.}
$\hat\vC_t\equiv\vC$ (known/oracle readout) recovers a fixed projection; $\vpi_t:=\vy_t$ with
$\vC$ learned by $\partial\hat{\mathcal L}/\partial\vC$ recovers the original VJF. Per-step
cost stays $O(nm)$ (same order as the decoder), preserving real-time complexity.

\paragraph{Empirical results (limit-cycle Poisson benchmark).}
On a $1000$\,s log-linear Poisson limit cycle ($5$\,ms bins, mean ${\sim}20$\,Hz,
$T{=}200{,}000$; $n{=}50/150/250$ neurons $\to{\sim}3/6/8$\,dB), filtered-latent $R^2$ is:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\hline
recognition input / readout & $3$\,dB & $6$\,dB & $8$\,dB\\
\hline
proj.\ enc., oracle $\vC$ (ceiling)             & 0.86 & 0.94 & 0.96\\
spike enc., oracle $\vC$ (raw-VJF bound)        & 0.69 & 0.84 & 0.91\\
proj.\ enc., online $\hat\vC_t$ ($\tau{=}8$)    & \textbf{0.84} & 0.86 & 0.85\\
proj.\ enc., online $\hat\vC_t$ ($\tau{=}1$)    & 0.73 & 0.84 & 0.88\\
spike enc., frozen PCA $\vC$                     & 0.72 & \textbf{0.87} & \textbf{0.92}\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\textbf{(1)} The projection input is a clear, robust win at \emph{fixed} $\vC$: projection-oracle
dominates spike-oracle everywhere, most strongly on one-step prediction
($0.84/0.95/0.97$ vs.\ $0.60/0.85/0.92$) --- confirming that $\vpi_t=\vC^+(\tilde g(\vy_t)-\vb)$
spares $h$ the $n\!\to\!m$ inversion and shrinks its input from $\R^n$ to $\R^m$
($2$ vs.\ $50$--$250$). \textbf{(2)} The \emph{online} readout reaches the
projection ceiling only at low SNR ($3$\,dB: $0.84$ vs.\ frozen-PCA $0.72$, spike-oracle $0.69$);
at high SNR it underperforms even a well-initialized frozen PCA ($0.85$ vs.\ $0.92$ at $8$\,dB)
and lags its own oracle ceiling. The periodic Procrustes refresh keeps moving the latent frame,
so $f$ fits a moving target: online one-step $R^2$ ($0.58/0.71/0.71$) and forecast horizon
($k{=}11/82/100$) trail the fixed-$\vC$ modes. \textbf{(3)} Smoothing helps the noisy regime and
rate fidelity (rate corr.\ $0.84/0.88/0.87$ at $\tau{=}8$ vs.\ $0.54/0.71/0.76$ at $\tau{=}1$) but
adds lag that mildly hurts high-SNR latent $R^2$. Per-bin online cost is flat ${\sim}3$\,ms
($50\!\to\!250$ neurons), real-time at $5$\,ms bins.

\emph{Open problem.} Make $\hat\vC_t$ converge to the oracle subspace at high SNR with less frame
churn --- e.g.\ slower / annealed refresh, freezing once the subspace stabilizes, or rotating the
flow with $\vR^\star$ at each refresh so the dynamics track the frame exactly.
