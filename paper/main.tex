\begin{abstract}
Variational joint filtering (VJF) learns a latent nonlinear dynamical system from neural
population spiking and infers its state online, one observation at a time, which makes it
attractive for closed-loop neuroscience. Two obstacles keep it from turnkey real-time use.
First, the mapping from latent states to firing rates --- the readout --- is usually unknown, and
the obvious remedy of learning it by the same variational gradient fails under sparse, low-rate
spikes: most bins carry almost no signal, so the estimate collapses to explaining only the
average firing rate. Second, the control flow that makes online learning behave --- a warm-up
before the dynamics are trusted, recovery from numerical divergence, and a stable recursive fit
over long streams --- is subtle and easily mis-implemented. We address both with changes that
leave the generative model and the filtering objective untouched: a \emph{projection encoder}
that feeds the recognition network the readout's own low-dimensional approximate inverse rather
than the raw spikes; a \emph{decoupled online readout} estimated by incremental principal
component analysis instead of the variational gradient; and a \emph{square-root recursive
least-squares flow learner} wrapped in a reusable per-sample loop. On a 1000-second, biologically
calibrated Poisson limit-cycle benchmark the method runs in about three milliseconds per
five-millisecond bin on a single CPU --- real time --- and recovers the latent dynamics with the
readout unknown. \emph{The projection encoder is a robust, readout-agnostic improvement; the
decoupled online readout reaches the known-readout accuracy only at low signal-to-noise, where
the periodic frame realignment does least harm to the learned dynamics.} We attribute the
high-SNR shortfall to that frame motion and outline two concrete remedies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Reading the moment-to-moment state of a neural population as the spikes arrive is the enabling
step for closed-loop experiments and adaptive neural interfaces: the controller must estimate,
predict, and act within a single time bin. Offline latent-variable models of population activity
are now mature --- recent nonlinear state-space methods such as XFADS \autocite{Dowling2024b}
learn highly predictive generative models by approximate variational smoothing over a whole
recording --- but few operate in this streaming, act-now regime. Variational joint filtering
(VJF) is an exception \autocite{zhao2020variational}: it pairs an amortized recognition network
\autocite{kingma2014auto} with a recursive estimate of a nonlinear state-space model, updating
both the posterior and the model once per time bin.

Two obstacles stand between VJF and a turnkey real-time tool. The first is the unknown readout.
Learning a readout from sparse spikes online is like calibrating a camera in a nearly dark room:
most frames carry too few photons to constrain anything, so an estimator that merely follows the
per-frame gradient defaults to reporting the average brightness and never recovers the scene. The
variational gradient on the readout is starved in exactly this way under low firing rates and
collapses to a trivial mean-rate solution. We instead estimate the readout with a dedicated,
well-conditioned online rule, and we hand the recognition network the readout's own approximate
inverse --- a few latent coordinates --- rather than the full, mostly-silent spike vector it
would otherwise have to invert. The second obstacle is mundane but real: driving VJF online
correctly means threading the posterior across bins, holding the dynamics fixed during an initial
warm-up, recovering gracefully from numerical divergence, and keeping the recursive dynamics fit
stable over hundreds of thousands of steps. In released code this logic lives only inside
experiment scripts.

Here we introduce three coupled changes (\cref{sec:method}): a \emph{projection encoder}, a
\emph{decoupled online readout}, and a \emph{square-root RLS flow learner} packaged in a reusable
real-time loop. The first two resolve the unknown-readout obstacle; the third resolves the
control-flow obstacle. Throughout, the generative model and the filtering objective of
\autocite{zhao2020variational} are unchanged, and we emphasize precisely what differs from
vanilla VJF. We validate on a biologically calibrated Poisson limit cycle (\cref{sec:exp,sec:results})
and give a calibrated verdict: the projection encoder is a robust win at any fixed readout, while
the online readout matches the known-readout accuracy only at low SNR.

\section{Background: variational joint filtering}\label{sec:bg}
With latent $\vx_t\in\reals^m$, observation $\vy_t\in\reals^n$, and optional input $\vu_t$, VJF
assumes a point-process / GLM observation and an additive radial-basis-function (RBF) dynamics,
\begin{align}
  \vy_t &\sim P\!\big(g(\vC\vx_t+\vb)\big), \qquad g=\exp, \tag{lik}\label{eq:lik}\\
  \vx_{t+1} &= \vx_t + f(\vx_t) + \vB_t\vu_t + \veps_{t+1},\quad
  f(\vx)=\vW\vphi(\vx),\quad \veps_t\sim\mathcal N(\bm0,\sigma^2\vI), \tag{gen}\label{eq:gen}
\end{align}
where $P$ is the Poisson (point-process) likelihood, $\vC\in\reals^{n\times m}$ and $\vb$ are the
\emph{readout} (the observation model, realized as a linear decoder), and the flow $f$ is a linear
combination $\vW\vphi$ of RBF features $\vphi$ with centers $\{\vxi_i\}$. A recognition network
$h$ produces the recursive Gaussian posterior $q(\vx_t)=\mathcal N(\vmu_t,\vs_t)$, with $\vs_t$
the per-dimension variance, and all parameters $\theta$ are learned by maximizing the per-step
filtering ELBO,
\begin{align}
  [\vmu_t,\vs_t] &= h(\vy_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1}), \tag{recog}\label{eq:recog}\\
  \hat{\mathcal L} &= \log p(\vy_t\mid\tilde\vx_t,\theta)
     + \E_{q(\vx_t)}\log p(\vx_t\mid\tilde\vx_{t-1},\theta) + H(q(\vx_t)), \tag{ELBO}\label{eq:elbo}
\end{align}
where $\tilde\vx_t$ is a reparameterized sample from $q(\vx_t)$ and $H$ is the entropy. The readout
is identifiable only up to an invertible $\vR$, since $\vC\vx_t=(\vC\vR)(\vR^{-1}\vx_t)$;
\autocite{zhao2020variational} initializes $\vC$ by factor analysis and column-wise
$\ell_2$-normalizes it every iteration, which fixes the column scale but leaves the
rotation/reflection free. \Cref{alg:vjf} reproduces the original single-step VJF procedure,
our reference point: it feeds the raw $\vy_t$ to $h$ and updates \emph{all} parameters $\bm\Theta$
--- including the readout $\vC,\vb$ --- by one reparameterized ELBO gradient.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Vanilla VJF, single step (reproduced from \autocite{zhao2020variational}).}
\label{alg:vjf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{Vjf}{$\vy_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1},\bm\Theta$}
  \State $\veps_t,\veps_{t-1}\sim\mathcal N(\bm0,\vI)$ \Comment{draw samples}
  \State $[\vmu_t,\vs_t]\coloneqq h(\vy_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1})$ \Comment{recognition on raw $\vy_t$}
  \State $\tilde\vx_t\coloneqq\vmu_t+\vs_t^{1/2}\veps_t$;\quad $\tilde\vx_{t-1}\coloneqq\vmu_{t-1}+\vs_{t-1}^{1/2}\veps_{t-1}$
  \State update \emph{all} $\bm\Theta$ (incl.\ $\vC,\vb$) by $\nabla_{\bm\Theta}\hat{\mathcal L}(\bm\Theta;\vy_t,\tilde\vx_t,\tilde\vx_{t-1},\vu_{t-1})$ \Comment{model update}
  \State \Return $\vmu_t,\vs_t,\bm\Theta$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
We make three coupled changes (\cref{ssec:proj,ssec:readout,ssec:srrls,ssec:loop}); the generative
model \cref{eq:lik,eq:gen} and the objective \cref{eq:elbo} are untouched. Vanilla VJF is
recovered by setting the recognition input to the raw $\vy_t$ and learning $\vC$ by
$\partial\hat{\mathcal L}/\partial\vC$.

\subsection{Projection encoder}\label{ssec:proj}
The recognition map \cref{eq:recog} must invert $n$ sparse channels into $m$ latent dimensions,
which is hard to learn at low SNR. We replace the raw observation by a deterministic,
$\vC$-dependent feature: the least-squares projection of a link-matched observation feature onto
the current loading subspace,
\begin{align}
  \vpi_t &= \vC^{+}\,(\tilde g(\vy_t)-\vb),\qquad
     \vC^{+}\in\reals^{m\times n}\ \text{the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse}, \label{eq:proj}\\
  [\vmu_t,\vs_t] &= h(\vpi_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1}). \tag{recog$'$}
\end{align}
($\vC^{+}=(\vC\trp\vC)^{-1}\vC\trp$ when $\vC$ has full column rank.) Here $\tilde g\approx g^{-1}$
is a variance-stabilizing, approximate inverse link. For the point-process case
$\tilde g(\vy_t)=\log(\vnu_t+c)$ with a small pseudocount $c$ and a causal exponential moving
average (EMA) of the counts,
\begin{equation}
  \vnu_t = (1-\alpha)\,\vnu_{t-1}+\alpha\,\vy_t,\qquad \alpha=1/\tau, \tag{EMA}\label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
(memory $\sim\tau$ bins; $\tau{=}1$ disables smoothing), and $\tilde g=\mathrm{id}$ for Gaussian
observations. \textbf{Difference from VJF:} the recognition input shrinks from the
high-dimensional spike vector ($\reals^n$) to a low-dimensional latent coordinate ($\reals^m$,
$m\!\ll\!n$); the network never sees the raw, mostly-silent spikes, only the readout's own
approximate inverse, and need only perform an $m\!\to\!m$ correction. Because $q(\vx_t)$ may be
any data-dependent distribution, parameterizing it through $\vpi_t$ leaves the bound
\cref{eq:elbo} valid; only the amortization family changes. The pseudoinverse $\vC^{+}$ is
recomputed only when the readout is refreshed (\cref{ssec:readout}) and reused as one $O(nm)$ map
per bin. The timescale $\tau$ is a fixed hyperparameter ($\tau\ll$ the dynamics timescale), not
ELBO-learned.

\subsection{Decoupled online readout}\label{ssec:readout}
Estimating $\vC$ by $\partial\hat{\mathcal L}/\partial\vC$ under sparse low-rate Poisson collapses
to the trivial mean-rate solution: the gradient is scaled by the per-bin rate
$g(\vC\vx_t+\vb)\!\approx\!10^{-1}$, so $\vC$ barely moves and a basin with $\vC\!\to\!0$ absorbs
the loss. \textbf{Difference from VJF:} we replace this gradient by a decoupled online
second-moment estimator. Let $\vl_t=\tilde g(\vy_t)$ and $\bar\vb_t$ its running mean, which we use
as the bias $\vb$ in \cref{eq:lik}. Intuitively, the readout subspace is just the top principal
subspace of the smoothed log-rate, which we can track online without ever forming a covariance.
Concretely we use candid covariance-free incremental PCA (CCIPCA) \autocite{weng2003candid} on the
centered feature $\vr_t=\vl_t-\bar\vb_t$ (distinct from the control $\vu_t$): maintaining vectors
$\vv_1,\dots,\vv_m$ that converge to $\lambda_i\ve_i$ (eigenvalue $\times$ eigenvector of
$\E[\vr\vr\trp]$). With $\vr_t^{(1)}=\vr_t$ and rate $\eta_t=1/t$, for $i=1,\dots,m$,
\begin{align}
  \vv_i &\leftarrow (1-\eta_t)\,\vv_i + \eta_t\,\big(\vr_t^{(i)\trp}\hat\vv_i\big)\,\vr_t^{(i)},
     \qquad \hat\vv_i=\vv_i/\norm{\vv_i}, \tag{Hebb}\label{eq:hebb}\\
  \vr_t^{(i+1)} &= \vr_t^{(i)} - \big(\vr_t^{(i)\trp}\hat\vv_i\big)\,\hat\vv_i. \tag{deflate}\label{eq:deflate}
\end{align}
Each component is an online power iteration on the covariance
($\E[(\vr\trp\hat\vv_i)\vr]=\E[\vr\vr\trp]\hat\vv_i$) followed by Gram--Schmidt deflation so that
component $i{+}1$ sees only the orthogonal complement. At convergence $\norm{\vv_i}=\lambda_i$ and
the loading column is $\vc_i=\hat\vv_i\sqrt{\norm{\vv_i}}=\ve_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ (unit-variance
latent). This is a well-conditioned M-step, free of the rate-scaled-gradient collapse, and shared
by the decoder \cref{eq:lik} and the projection \cref{eq:proj}; it is a streaming analogue of the
PLDS/GPFA-style readout warm-start \autocite{macke2011empirical,yu2009gaussian}. The estimate
$\hat\vC_t$ is written to the decoder every $K$ steps.

\paragraph{Identifiability / normalization.}
The column-$\ell_2$ normalization of \autocite{zhao2020variational} fixes only the scale gauge.
Our setting needs more: each incremental-PCA refresh returns a basis with an arbitrary rotation
and sign (eigenvectors are signed; near-degenerate eigenvalues rotate freely). We therefore fix
the full orthogonal gauge by orthogonal Procrustes alignment of the refreshed loading to the
previous one,
\begin{equation}
  \vR^\star=\arg\min_{\vR\trp\vR=\vI}\norm{\hat\vC\vR-\vC_{\mathrm{prev}}}_F=\vU\vV\trp,\qquad
  \hat\vC\trp\vC_{\mathrm{prev}}=\vU\vSig\vV\trp\ (\text{SVD}), \label{eq:procrustes}
\end{equation}
and fix the scale by the unit-variance column scaling above. This removes the sign/rotation jumps
between refreshes and keeps the latent frame approximately fixed; the residual frame motion, and
its effect on the learned dynamics $f=\vW\vphi$, is taken up in \cref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Square-root RLS flow learner}\label{ssec:srrls}
Over a long online pass the recursive least-squares (RLS) fit of the RBF velocity weights
accumulates error in the precision matrix and eventually diverges. \textbf{Difference from VJF:}
we propagate a covariance square-root factor $\vS$ ($\vP=\vS\vS\trp$) directly (square-root RLS,
\autocite{haykin2014adaptive,schoenberg1983implementation}), avoiding the precision wind-up that
destabilizes plain RLS over long ($\sim2\times10^5$-step) streams; stochastic-gradient flow is
stable but slower to converge.

\subsection{The real-time loop}\label{ssec:loop}
The three changes above plug into a single per-sample driver (\cref{alg:rtvjf}), so a real-time
consumer calls one function per arriving sample. The loop threads the posterior across bins; holds
the dynamics frozen during a warm-up of $W$ bins while the recognition settles, then initializes
$f$ once from the buffered latent means and narrows the RBF widths by $\rho$; recovers from
divergence with a state-noise floor $\sigma^2_{\min}$ and a state-magnitude bound $B$ (repeating
the last good mean); and refreshes the readout every $K$ steps. The new hyperparameters are
exactly $(W,K,\tau,\rho,\sigma^2_{\min},B)$. The projection and readout add $O(nm)$ per step (the
same order as the decoder); the total per-step cost also includes the recognition network and the
square-root-RLS update.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Real-time VJF with projection encoder and decoupled online readout (one pass).}
\label{alg:rtvjf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require stream $\{\vy_t\}$; warm-up $W$; refresh interval $K$; smoothing $\tau$ ($\alpha{=}1/\tau$);
  width scale $\rho$; noise floor $\sigma^2_{\min}$; state bound $B$; pseudocount $c$
\State init VJF (recognition $h$, flow $\vW$, decoder $(\vC,\vb)$); readout state $\vnu,\bar\vb,\{\vv_i\}$; $q\gets\varnothing$; buffer $\mathcal B\gets[\,]$
\For{$t=0,1,2,\dots$}
  \If{$t = W$} \State initialize flow $f$ from $\mathcal B$; scale RBF widths by $\rho$ \Comment{turn dynamics on, once} \EndIf
  \State $\vnu \gets (1-\alpha)\,\vnu + \alpha\,\vy_t$;\ \ $\vl \gets \log(\vnu+c)$;\ \ update $\bar\vb$ \Comment{link-matched feature, \cref{eq:ema}}
  \State CCIPCA step on $\vl-\bar\vb$;\ \ $\vpi \gets \vC^{+}(\vl-\bar\vb)$ \Comment{decoupled readout + projection, \cref{eq:hebb,eq:proj}}
  \State $\vmu_t, q \gets \textsc{VjfFilter}(\vy_t,\vpi,q;\ \textsc{learnDyn}{=}[t\ge W])$ \Comment{\cref{alg:vjf} on $\vpi$, with $\vC,\vb$ held fixed; decode on $\vy_t$}
  \State clamp $\log\sigma^2 \ge \log\sigma^2_{\min}$ \Comment{state-noise floor}
  \If{$\vmu_t$ non-finite \textbf{or} $\norm{\vmu_t}_\infty > B$} \State $q\gets\varnothing$;\ \ $\vmu_t\gets$ last good mean \Comment{divergence guard} \EndIf
  \If{$t < W$} \State append $\vmu_t$ to $\mathcal B$ \EndIf
  \If{$t>0$ \textbf{and} $t \bmod K = 0$} \State $\hat\vC_t\gets$ Procrustes-align to $\vC$ (\cref{eq:procrustes}); write $(\hat\vC_t,\bar\vb)$ to decoder; recompute $\vC^{+}$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{What changed.} Relative to vanilla VJF (\cref{alg:vjf}), the loop (\cref{alg:rtvjf})
differs in exactly three places: the recognition reads the projection $\vpi_t$ rather than the raw
$\vy_t$; the readout $\vC,\vb$ is removed from the ELBO gradient and instead estimated by CCIPCA
with a Procrustes anchor (so the gradient step trains only the recognition, flow, and biases); and
a warm-up, a state-noise floor, and a divergence guard make the single step safe to iterate for
$10^5$ bins. Everything else --- the recognition network, the reparameterized ELBO gradient on the
remaining parameters, and the posterior threading --- is the vanilla update.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
We use a self-contained synthetic benchmark: a 2-D limit cycle (radius-relaxing, constant angular
velocity) observed through a sparse log-linear Poisson population, calibrated to biological rates
(mean $\sim20$\,Hz, peak $\le100$\,Hz at 5\,ms bins). One single-trial stream of $T=200{,}000$
bins (1000\,s) is generated; signal-to-noise --- the Fisher-information SNR of the latent
trajectory \autocite{Jeon2024b} --- is set by population size, giving three conditions:
$n=50/150/250$ neurons, corresponding to $\sim3/6/8$\,dB (\cref{fig:raster}). We compare five
configurations, crossing the recognition input (spike vs.\ projection encoder) with how the
readout is obtained: oracle (true $\vC$); the decoupled online readout of \cref{ssec:readout}; or
a frozen batch-PCA readout fit once on the first $60n$ bins (e.g.\ $15{,}000$ bins at $n{=}250$).
These give spike+oracle (the vanilla-VJF bound), projection+oracle (the projection ceiling),
projection+online ($\tau{=}8$ and $\tau{=}1$), and spike+frozen-PCA. The dynamics use the
square-root RLS flow learner throughout. We report the filtered-latent $R^2$ (best affine image of
the posterior mean against the true latent), one-step prediction $R^2$, rate correlation, free-run
forecast horizon (largest $k$ with $R^2\!\ge\!0$), and the steady per-bin compute time.

reaches the known-readout ceiling only at low SNR, where the periodic-refresh frame motion does
least harm to the learned dynamics; and smoothing buys rate fidelity in the noisy regime.}

\textbf{(1) The projection input is a clear, robust win at fixed readout.} Projection+oracle beats
spike+oracle at every SNR (\cref{tab:r2}), most strongly on one-step prediction
($0.83/0.95/0.97$ vs.\ $0.60/0.85/0.92$; \cref{tab:dyn}): feeding the recognition network the
readout's own approximate inverse spares it the high-dimensional, sparse inversion.
\textbf{(2) The online readout reaches the ceiling, in filtered-latent $R^2$, only at low SNR.}
At $3$\,dB the online estimate ($\tau{=}8$) comes within $0.02$ of the projection+oracle ceiling
($0.84$ vs.\ $0.86$; well above frozen-PCA $0.72$ and spike-oracle $0.69$). At $8$\,dB it trails
even a frozen PCA ($0.85$ vs.\ $0.92$) and its own ceiling. Crucially, even at $3$\,dB it does not
lead on the dynamics metrics: its one-step $R^2$ ($0.58/0.71/0.71$) and forecast horizon
($k{=}11/82/100$, \cref{tab:dyn}, \cref{fig:kstep}) trail the fixed-readout modes, and the online
estimate is still climbing at $1000$\,s for high SNR (\cref{fig:curves}).
\textbf{(3) Smoothing helps the noisy regime and rate fidelity.} Rate correlation is
$0.84/0.88/0.87$ at $\tau{=}8$ vs.\ $0.54/0.71/0.76$ at $\tau{=}1$ (\cref{tab:dyn}); the EMA
denoises the readout feature at the cost of a small lag that mildly reduces high-SNR
filtered-latent $R^2$.

Per-bin online cost is flat at $\sim3$\,ms from 50 to 250 neurons on a single CPU core --- real
time at $5$\,ms bins, closed-loop feasible --- with zero divergences over the $1000$\,s pass. At
good SNR the learned flow forecasts $\sim2.4$ cycles ahead (the periodic orbit has period
$\sim42$ bins; $k{=}100$, \cref{fig:forecast}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
With an unknown observation model, real-time VJF is feasible: a cheap small-window readout
warm-start plus the projection encoder recovers the latent dynamics online at biological rates and
real-time cost, and the projection encoder is a robust, SNR-graded improvement over raw-spike
recognition even when the readout is known. \emph{The projection encoder is a readout-agnostic
win; the decoupled online readout reaches the known-readout ceiling only at low SNR.} The
remaining gap is the online readout at high SNR, where it does not reach its own
(projection+oracle) ceiling and is beaten by a frozen batch-PCA readout. We attribute this to the
periodic Procrustes refresh: each rewrite of the readout shifts the latent coordinate frame, so
the recursive dynamics learner must chase a moving target, degrading one-step prediction and
forecast horizon even when the filtered-latent estimate is accurate. An ablation that freezes the
refresh once the subspace stabilizes would isolate this mechanism; we leave it, and two concrete
remedies, to future work: anneal or freeze the refresh after convergence, or rotate the RBF flow
by $\vR^\star$ at each refresh (exact for a rotation: the RBF centers
$\vxi_i\mapsto\vR^\star\vxi_i$, with $\vW$ transformed accordingly) so the dynamics track the
frame.

These results are, to our knowledge, the first demonstration of real-time VJF with an unknown
readout; the scope is, however, a single synthetic benchmark (one limit-cycle trajectory class,
log-linear Poisson observations, no real data). Validating on recorded population activity and on
richer dynamics is the natural next step.
