\begin{abstract}
Variational joint filtering (VJF) learns a latent nonlinear dynamical system from neural
population spiking and infers its state online, one observation at a time, which makes it
attractive for closed-loop neuroscience. Two obstacles keep it from turnkey real-time use.
First, the mapping from latent states to firing rates --- the readout --- is usually unknown, and
the obvious remedy of learning it by the same variational gradient fails under sparse, low-rate
spikes: most bins carry almost no signal, so the estimate collapses to explaining only the
average firing rate. Second, the control flow that makes online learning behave --- a warm-up
before the dynamics are trusted, recovery from numerical divergence, and a stable recursive fit
over long streams --- is subtle and easily mis-implemented. We address both with changes that
leave the generative model and the filtering objective untouched, and package them as \svjf\
(\svjflong): a \emph{projection encoder} that feeds the recognition network the readout's own
low-dimensional approximate inverse rather than the raw spikes; a \emph{warm-started,
two-timescale online readout} estimated by incremental principal component analysis --- a fast
per-bin update and a slow periodic write --- instead of the variational gradient; and a
\emph{square-root recursive least-squares flow learner}, all wrapped in a reusable per-sample
loop. On a 1000-second, biologically calibrated Poisson limit-cycle benchmark spanning the
signal-to-noise range of population recordings ($-3$ to $8$\,dB), \svjf\ runs in about three
milliseconds per five-millisecond bin on a single CPU --- real time --- and recovers the latent
dynamics with the readout unknown. \emph{The gains concentrate in the low-SNR regime typical of
neural data: there the projection encoder and the online readout recover dynamics that raw-spike
recognition cannot, whereas at high SNR the problem is easy enough that even a frozen
principal-component readout is near-oracle.} Once the online readout has converged, freezing or
annealing its refresh recovers forecast accuracy that continued rewriting would otherwise erode.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Reading the moment-to-moment state of a neural population as the spikes arrive is the enabling
step for closed-loop experiments and adaptive neural interfaces: the controller must estimate,
predict, and act within a single time bin. Offline latent-variable models of population activity
are now mature --- recent nonlinear state-space methods such as XFADS \autocite{Dowling2024b}
learn highly predictive generative models by approximate variational smoothing over a whole
recording --- but few operate in this streaming, act-now regime. Variational joint filtering
(VJF) is an exception \autocite{zhao2020variational}: it pairs an amortized recognition network
\autocite{kingma2014auto} with a recursive estimate of a nonlinear state-space model, updating
both the posterior and the model once per time bin.

Two obstacles stand between VJF and a turnkey real-time tool. The first is the unknown readout.
Learning a readout from sparse spikes online is like calibrating a camera in a nearly dark room:
most frames carry too few photons to constrain anything, so an estimator that merely follows the
per-frame gradient defaults to reporting the average brightness and never recovers the scene. The
variational gradient on the readout is starved in exactly this way under low firing rates and
collapses to a trivial mean-rate solution. We instead estimate the readout with a dedicated,
well-conditioned online rule, and we hand the recognition network the readout's own approximate
inverse --- a few latent coordinates --- rather than the full, mostly-silent spike vector it
would otherwise have to invert. The second obstacle is mundane but real: driving VJF online
correctly means threading the posterior across bins, holding the dynamics fixed during an initial
warm-up, recovering gracefully from numerical divergence, and keeping the recursive dynamics fit
stable over hundreds of thousands of steps. In released code this logic lives only inside
experiment scripts.

Here we introduce \svjf\ (\svjflong), three coupled changes (\cref{sec:method}) packaged in a
reusable real-time loop: a \emph{projection encoder}, a \emph{warm-started two-timescale online
readout}, and a \emph{square-root RLS flow learner}. The first two resolve the unknown-readout
obstacle; the third resolves the control-flow obstacle. Throughout, the generative model and the
filtering objective of \autocite{zhao2020variational} are unchanged, and we emphasize precisely
what differs from vanilla VJF. We validate on a biologically calibrated Poisson limit cycle across
the signal-to-noise range of population recordings ($-3$ to $8$\,dB; \cref{sec:exp,sec:results})
and give a calibrated verdict: the contributions earn their keep in the \emph{low-SNR} regime
typical of neural data --- few neurons, sparse spikes --- where readout estimation is hard; at
high SNR the problem is easy enough that a frozen principal-component readout is already
near-oracle. The projection encoder is a robust win at any fixed readout, and once the online
readout converges, freezing or annealing its refresh recovers forecast accuracy that continued
rewriting erodes.

\section{Background: variational joint filtering}\label{sec:bg}
With latent $\vx_t\in\reals^m$, observation $\vy_t\in\reals^n$, and optional input $\vu_t$, VJF
assumes a point-process / GLM observation and an additive radial-basis-function (RBF) dynamics,
\begin{align}
  \vy_t &\sim P\!\big(g(\vC\vx_t+\vb)\big), \qquad g=\exp, \tag{lik}\label{eq:lik}\\
  \vx_{t+1} &= \vx_t + f(\vx_t) + \vB_t\vu_t + \veps_{t+1},\quad
  f(\vx)=\vW\vphi(\vx),\quad \veps_t\sim\mathcal N(\bm0,\sigma^2\vI), \tag{gen}\label{eq:gen}
\end{align}
where $P$ is the Poisson (point-process) likelihood, $\vC\in\reals^{n\times m}$ and $\vb$ are the
\emph{readout} (the observation model, realized as a linear decoder), and the flow $f$ is a linear
combination $\vW\vphi$ of RBF features $\vphi$ with centers $\{\vxi_i\}$. A recognition network
$h$ produces the recursive Gaussian posterior $q(\vx_t)=\mathcal N(\vmu_t,\vs_t)$, with $\vs_t$
the per-dimension variance, and all parameters $\theta$ are learned by maximizing the per-step
filtering ELBO,
\begin{align}
  [\vmu_t,\vs_t] &= h(\vy_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1}), \tag{recog}\label{eq:recog}\\
  \hat{\mathcal L} &= \log p(\vy_t\mid\tilde\vx_t,\theta)
     + \E_{q(\vx_t)}\log p(\vx_t\mid\tilde\vx_{t-1},\theta) + H(q(\vx_t)), \tag{ELBO}\label{eq:elbo}
\end{align}
where $\tilde\vx_t$ is a reparameterized sample from $q(\vx_t)$ and $H$ is the entropy. The readout
is identifiable only up to an invertible $\vR$, since $\vC\vx_t=(\vC\vR)(\vR^{-1}\vx_t)$;
\autocite{zhao2020variational} initializes $\vC$ by factor analysis and column-wise
$\ell_2$-normalizes it every iteration, which fixes the column scale but leaves the
rotation/reflection free. \Cref{alg:vjf} reproduces the original single-step VJF procedure,
our reference point: it feeds the raw $\vy_t$ to $h$ and updates \emph{all} parameters $\bm\Theta$
--- including the readout $\vC,\vb$ --- by one reparameterized ELBO gradient.

\begin{algorithm}[!ht]
\caption{Vanilla VJF, single step (reproduced from \autocite{zhao2020variational}).}
\label{alg:vjf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{Vjf}{$\vy_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1},\bm\Theta$}
  \State $\veps_t,\veps_{t-1}\sim\mathcal N(\bm0,\vI)$ \Comment{draw samples}
  \State $[\vmu_t,\vs_t]\coloneqq h(\vy_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1})$ \Comment{recognition on raw $\vy_t$}
  \State $\tilde\vx_t\coloneqq\vmu_t+\vs_t^{1/2}\veps_t$;\quad $\tilde\vx_{t-1}\coloneqq\vmu_{t-1}+\vs_{t-1}^{1/2}\veps_{t-1}$
  \State update \emph{all} $\bm\Theta$ (incl.\ $\vC,\vb$) by $\nabla_{\bm\Theta}\hat{\mathcal L}(\bm\Theta;\vy_t,\tilde\vx_t,\tilde\vx_{t-1},\vu_{t-1})$ \Comment{model update}
  \State \Return $\vmu_t,\vs_t,\bm\Theta$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{The readout bottleneck.} Run as is on a streaming, low-SNR recording with the
readout unknown, vanilla VJF does not get off the ground, and the reason is specifically the
readout. \Cref{fig:motivation} streams the original procedure (\cref{alg:vjf}: raw-spike
recognition; readout $\vC$ and flow $\vW$ learned by the ELBO gradient) once through a $3$\,dB
Poisson limit-cycle, crossing the two optimizers (Adam, SGD) with two readout initializations
(random, oracle), against \svjf. With a \emph{random} readout initialization the latent is never
recovered --- one-step $R^2$ stalls at $0.2$--$0.4$ --- because the readout gradient is scaled by
the per-bin firing rate $g(\vC\vx_t+\vb)\!\approx\!10^{-1}$ (\cref{ssec:readout}), so $\vC$ slides
to a mean-rate solution while the recognition network, handed the raw, mostly-silent spikes, cannot
invert it. Hand the \emph{same} model the \emph{oracle} readout at initialization and it converges
to $R^2\!\approx\!0.94$ --- pinpointing the unknown readout, not the dynamics, as the obstacle.
\svjf\ reaches $R^2\!\approx\!0.93$ from spikes alone, recovering what an oracle readout would give
without one.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
We make three coupled changes (\cref{ssec:proj,ssec:readout,ssec:srrls,ssec:loop}); the generative
model \cref{eq:lik,eq:gen} and the objective \cref{eq:elbo} are untouched. Vanilla VJF is
recovered by setting the recognition input to the raw $\vy_t$ and learning $\vC$ by
$\partial\hat{\mathcal L}/\partial\vC$.

\subsection{Projection encoder}\label{ssec:proj}
The recognition map \cref{eq:recog} must invert $n$ sparse channels into $m$ latent dimensions,
which is hard to learn at low SNR. We replace the raw observation by a deterministic,
$\vC$-dependent feature: the least-squares projection of a link-matched observation feature onto
the current loading subspace,
\begin{align}
  \vpi_t &= \vC^{+}\,(\tilde g(\vy_t)-\vb),\qquad
     \vC^{+}\in\reals^{m\times n}\ \text{the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse}, \label{eq:proj}\\
  [\vmu_t,\vs_t] &= h(\vpi_t,\vu_{t-1},\vmu_{t-1},\vs_{t-1}). \tag{recog$'$}
\end{align}
($\vC^{+}=(\vC\trp\vC)^{-1}\vC\trp$ when $\vC$ has full column rank.) Here $\tilde g\approx g^{-1}$
is a variance-stabilizing, approximate inverse link. For the point-process case
$\tilde g(\vy_t)=\log(\vnu_t+c)$ with a small pseudocount $c$ and a causal exponential moving
average (EMA) of the counts,
\begin{equation}
  \vnu_t = (1-\alpha)\,\vnu_{t-1}+\alpha\,\vy_t,\qquad \alpha=1/\tau, \tag{EMA}\label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
(memory $\sim\tau$ bins; $\tau{=}1$ disables smoothing), and $\tilde g=\mathrm{id}$ for Gaussian
observations. \textbf{Difference from VJF:} the recognition input shrinks from the
high-dimensional spike vector ($\reals^n$) to a low-dimensional latent coordinate ($\reals^m$,
$m\!\ll\!n$); the network never sees the raw, mostly-silent spikes, only the readout's own
approximate inverse, and need only perform an $m\!\to\!m$ correction. Because $q(\vx_t)$ may be
any data-dependent distribution, parameterizing it through $\vpi_t$ leaves the bound
\cref{eq:elbo} valid; only the amortization family changes. The pseudoinverse $\vC^{+}$ is
recomputed only when the readout is refreshed (\cref{ssec:readout}) and reused as one $O(nm)$ map
per bin. The timescale $\tau$ is a fixed hyperparameter ($\tau\ll$ the dynamics timescale), not
ELBO-learned.

\subsection{Decoupled online readout}\label{ssec:readout}
Estimating $\vC$ by $\partial\hat{\mathcal L}/\partial\vC$ under sparse low-rate Poisson collapses
to the trivial mean-rate solution: the gradient is scaled by the per-bin rate
$g(\vC\vx_t+\vb)\!\approx\!10^{-1}$, so $\vC$ barely moves and a basin with $\vC\!\to\!0$ absorbs
the loss. \textbf{Difference from VJF:} we replace this gradient by a decoupled online
second-moment estimator. Let $\vl_t=\tilde g(\vy_t)$ and $\bar\vb_t$ its running mean, which we use
as the bias $\vb$ in \cref{eq:lik}. Intuitively, the readout subspace is just the top principal
subspace of the smoothed log-rate, which we can track online without ever forming a covariance.
Concretely we use candid covariance-free incremental PCA (CCIPCA) \autocite{weng2003candid} on the
centered feature $\vr_t=\vl_t-\bar\vb_t$ (distinct from the control $\vu_t$): maintaining vectors
$\vv_1,\dots,\vv_m$ that converge to $\lambda_i\ve_i$ (eigenvalue $\times$ eigenvector of
$\E[\vr\vr\trp]$). With $\vr_t^{(1)}=\vr_t$ and rate $\eta_t=1/t$, for $i=1,\dots,m$,
\begin{align}
  \vv_i &\leftarrow (1-\eta_t)\,\vv_i + \eta_t\,\big(\vr_t^{(i)\trp}\hat\vv_i\big)\,\vr_t^{(i)},
     \qquad \hat\vv_i=\vv_i/\norm{\vv_i}, \tag{Hebb}\label{eq:hebb}\\
  \vr_t^{(i+1)} &= \vr_t^{(i)} - \big(\vr_t^{(i)\trp}\hat\vv_i\big)\,\hat\vv_i. \tag{deflate}\label{eq:deflate}
\end{align}
Each component is an online power iteration on the covariance
($\E[(\vr\trp\hat\vv_i)\vr]=\E[\vr\vr\trp]\hat\vv_i$) followed by Gram--Schmidt deflation so that
component $i{+}1$ sees only the orthogonal complement. At convergence $\norm{\vv_i}=\lambda_i$ and
the loading column is $\vc_i=\hat\vv_i\sqrt{\norm{\vv_i}}=\ve_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}$ (unit-variance
latent). This is a well-conditioned M-step, free of the rate-scaled-gradient collapse, and shared
by the decoder \cref{eq:lik} and the projection \cref{eq:proj}; it is a streaming analogue of the
PLDS/GPFA-style readout warm-start \autocite{macke2011empirical,yu2009gaussian}. We warm-start it
with a one-shot batch PCA on a short initial window (the first $60n$ bins), so the decoder is
sensible from the outset, and thereafter maintain it on \emph{two timescales}: a fast per-bin
CCIPCA update of $\{\vv_i\}$ (the inner loop), and a slow write of the aligned estimate
$\hat\vC_t$ to the decoder every $K$ bins (the outer loop). Decoupling the two lets the readout
adapt continuously while the decoder --- and with it the projection \cref{eq:proj} and the target
the dynamics learner must track --- changes only at the slow cadence.

\paragraph{Identifiability / normalization.}
The column-$\ell_2$ normalization of \autocite{zhao2020variational} fixes only the column scale.
Our setting needs more: the readout is identifiable only up to a rotation (factor-rotation
invariance), and each incremental-PCA refresh returns a basis with an arbitrary rotation and sign
(eigenvectors are signed; near-degenerate eigenvalues rotate freely), so the latent frame would
drift between refreshes. We therefore pin the rotation and reflection by orthogonal Procrustes
alignment of the refreshed loading to the previous one,
\begin{equation}
  \vR^\star=\arg\min_{\vR\trp\vR=\vI}\norm{\hat\vC\vR-\vC_{\mathrm{prev}}}_F=\vU\vV\trp,\qquad
  \hat\vC\trp\vC_{\mathrm{prev}}=\vU\vSig\vV\trp\ (\text{SVD}), \label{eq:procrustes}
\end{equation}
and fix the scale by the unit-variance column scaling above. This removes the sign/rotation jumps
between refreshes and keeps the latent frame essentially fixed --- empirically the tracked
subspace drifts by under a degree over the entire stream (\cref{sec:results}). The remaining cost
is subtler: each refresh still rewrites $\vC$ slightly, so the recursive dynamics learner fits
against a target that keeps moving. The two-timescale schedule below addresses this.

\paragraph{Freezing the refresh once converged (optional).}
Because the online readout converges well within a $1000$\,s stream, the slow outer loop can be
\emph{annealed or frozen} once the subspace stabilizes: after a settling horizon $S$ we stop (or
geometrically slow) the writes, fixing the decoder while the recognition, flow, and biases keep
adapting. This is the practical recipe for a stationary recording --- it removes the residual
target motion and, as \cref{sec:results} shows, recovers one-step prediction and forecast horizon
at no cost to filtering. Freezing is deliberately optional: in a non-stationary setting one wants
the readout to keep tracking, and detecting when to re-enable it is a question we leave open
(\cref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Square-root RLS flow learner}\label{ssec:srrls}
\textbf{Difference from VJF:} the original VJF learns \emph{every} parameter --- including the RBF
velocity weights $\vW$ --- by stochastic-gradient ascent on the ELBO \autocite{zhao2020variational},
which is stable but converges slowly online. Because $\vW$ enters the velocity linearly given the
latent path, we instead fit it by recursive least squares (RLS), which converges far faster. Plain
RLS, however, accumulates error in the propagated precision matrix and eventually diverges over a
long ($\sim2\times10^5$-step) pass, so we carry a covariance square-root factor $\vS$
($\vP=\vS\vS\trp$) directly (square-root RLS, \autocite{haykin2014adaptive,schoenberg1983implementation}):
this keeps RLS's fast convergence while staying numerically stable. \Cref{fig:flow} isolates the
flow learner (readout held fixed at the oracle, so only the flow varies): square-root
RLS converges fastest in the early stream and stays stable, whereas plain RLS diverges; gradient
learning (Adam, SGD) is competitive on accuracy but slower to converge --- SGD only catches up
later, to a marginally higher steady state. With a clean readout the flow learner is thus not a
large accuracy differentiator; we adopt square-root RLS for its early convergence and, decisively,
its numerical stability over long streams.

\subsection{The real-time loop}\label{ssec:loop}
The three changes above plug into a single per-sample driver (\cref{alg:rtvjf}), which a real-time
consumer calls once per arriving sample. It runs in two phases. During a \emph{warm-up} of $W$ bins
the dynamics are held off while the recognition settles and the latent means are buffered; at the
boundary the flow $f$ is initialized once from those means and the RBF widths are narrowed by
$\rho$. In the \emph{online} phase the loop threads the posterior across bins, updates the flow by
square-root RLS, and refreshes the readout every $K$ steps (which can optionally be frozen once
converged, \cref{ssec:readout}). Two numerical safeguards keep the per-bin update from blowing up
over the long stream: a floor $\sigma^2_{\min}$ on the posterior state variance (it cannot collapse
to zero) and a magnitude bound $B$ on the state (on a non-finite or out-of-bounds mean the
posterior is reset and the last good mean repeated). The new hyperparameters are exactly
$(W,K,\tau,\rho,\sigma^2_{\min},B,c)$ --- with $c$ the feature
pseudocount (\cref{fig:timescales} sketches the three timescales $W$, $K$, $\tau$). The projection
and readout add $O(nm)$ per step (the same order as the decoder); the total per-step cost also
includes the recognition network and the square-root-RLS update.

\begin{algorithm}[!ht]
\caption{\svjf: real-time VJF with projection encoder and decoupled online readout (one pass).}
\label{alg:rtvjf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require stream $\{\vy_t\}$; warm-up $W$; refresh interval $K$; smoothing $\tau$ ($\alpha{=}1/\tau$);
  width scale $\rho$; variance floor $\sigma^2_{\min}$; state bound $B$; pseudocount $c$
\State init VJF: recognition $h$, flow $f$, decoder $(\vC,\vb)$ from a short PCA warm-start
\State init readout state $\vnu,\bar\vb,\{\vv_i\}$;\ \ posterior $q\gets\varnothing$;\ \ buffer $\mathcal B\gets[\,]$
\Statex \textit{Phase 1 --- warm-up ($t=0,\dots,W{-}1$): dynamics off, collect latent means}
\For{$t=0$ \textbf{to} $W-1$}
  \State $\vnu \gets (1-\alpha)\,\vnu + \alpha\,\vy_t$ \Comment{EMA of the counts, \cref{eq:ema}}
  \State $\vl \gets \log(\vnu+c)$;\ \ update running mean $\bar\vb$ \Comment{link-matched feature}
  \State CCIPCA step on $\vl-\bar\vb$ \Comment{track the readout subspace, \cref{eq:hebb}}
  \State $\vpi \gets \vC^{+}(\vl-\bar\vb)$ \Comment{projection encoder, \cref{eq:proj}}
  \State $\vmu_t, q \gets \textsc{VjfFilter}(\vy_t,\vpi,q;\ \text{dynamics off})$ \Comment{recognition only; decode on $\vy_t$}
  \State append $\vmu_t$ to buffer $\mathcal B$
\EndFor
\State initialize flow $f$ from $\mathcal B$;\ \ scale RBF widths by $\rho$ \Comment{turn the dynamics on, once}
\Statex \textit{Phase 2 --- online ($t=W,W{+}1,\dots$): full joint filtering, in real time}
\For{$t=W,W+1,\dots$}
  \State compute feature $\vl$ and projection $\vpi$ from $\vy_t$ \Comment{EMA $+$ CCIPCA $+$ $\vC^{+}$, as in Phase 1}
  \State $\vmu_t, q \gets \textsc{VjfFilter}(\vy_t,\vpi,q;\ \text{dynamics on})$ \Comment{recognition + square-root-RLS flow update}
  \State clamp posterior $\log\sigma^2_t \ge \log\sigma^2_{\min}$ \Comment{variance floor}
  \If{$\vmu_t$ non-finite \textbf{or} $\norm{\vmu_t}_\infty > B$}
    \State $q\gets\varnothing$;\ \ $\vmu_t\gets$ last good mean \Comment{numerical divergence guard}
  \EndIf
  \If{$t \bmod K = 0$}
    \State refresh readout: Procrustes-align to $\vC$ (\cref{eq:procrustes}); write to decoder; recompute $\vC^{+}$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Differences from vanilla VJF.} Vanilla VJF (\cref{alg:vjf}) trains \emph{every} parameter by one
reparameterized ELBO-gradient step. \Cref{alg:rtvjf} changes three of them: the recognition reads
the projection $\vpi_t$ instead of the raw $\vy_t$; the readout $(\vC,\vb)$ is taken out of the
gradient and estimated by CCIPCA with a Procrustes anchor; and the flow weights $\vW$ are taken out
of the gradient and fit by square-root RLS. The ELBO-gradient step therefore trains only the
recognition network (and the remaining biases). A warm-up, a posterior-variance floor, and a
state-magnitude guard then make the single step safe to iterate for $10^5$ bins. Everything else
--- the generative model, the reparameterized bound, and the posterior threading --- is the vanilla
update.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}
We use a self-contained synthetic benchmark: a 2-D limit cycle (radius-relaxing, constant angular
velocity) observed through a sparse log-linear Poisson population, calibrated to biological rates
(mean $\sim20$\,Hz, peak $\le100$\,Hz at 5\,ms bins). One single-trial stream of $T=200{,}000$
bins (1000\,s) is generated; signal-to-noise --- the Fisher-information SNR of the latent
trajectory \autocite{Jeon2024b} --- is set by population size, giving five conditions that span
the neural regime: $n=15/30/50/150/250$ neurons, corresponding to $\sim-3/0/3/6/8$\,dB
(\cref{fig:raster}). \emph{This SNR is a property of the observation model only}: it measures how
well the instantaneous latent \emph{state} $\vx_t$ can be read from a single time bin (the Fisher
information of $\vx_t$ given $\vy_t$), and is set here by how many neurons report it. It is
\emph{not} the signal-to-noise of the \emph{dynamics} --- how identifiable the flow $f$ is from the
observed trajectory --- which is a distinct quantity (depending on the flow, the state noise, and
the trajectory's coverage of state space) that we do not attempt to quantify here. The distinction
is consequential: a regime can carry enough state SNR to filter the latent accurately yet too
little dynamics information to learn a flow that forecasts (\cref{sec:results}). The lower three ($-3/0/3$\,dB) are the sparse, few-neuron regime typical of
population recordings; the upper two are the easy end, included for completeness. We compare four
configurations, crossing the recognition input (spike vs.\ projection encoder) with how the
readout is obtained: an \emph{oracle readout} (the true loading $\vC$); the decoupled online
readout of \cref{ssec:readout} ($\tau{=}8$); or a frozen batch-PCA readout fit once on the first
$60n$ bins. These give
projection$+$oracle (the ceiling), projection$+$online (the achievable method), spike$+$oracle
(the vanilla-VJF bound), and spike$+$frozen-PCA. We additionally isolate the readout schedule of
\cref{ssec:readout} on a dedicated readout-stability experiment, comparing the oracle, a frozen
PCA, the slow/locked readout (frozen after a settling horizon $S$), and the fast,
continuously-rewriting online readout, at both high ($n{=}250$, $8$\,dB) and low ($n{=}30$,
$0$\,dB) SNR over three paired seeds. In \emph{every} configuration the dynamics flow $f$ is
learned online by the square-root-RLS learner --- no arm is handed the true dynamics --- so
throughout this paper \emph{oracle} refers to the readout $\vC$ alone (an oracle observation
model), never to the dynamical system. The oracle's forecast skill (\cref{sec:results}) is thus a
property of the \emph{learned} flow, fed by clean oracle-readout latents, not of a known $f$. We report the filtered-latent $R^2$, one-step prediction $R^2$, rate correlation, and the free-run
forecast horizon $H$. Because the latent is identifiable only up to an invertible linear map (the
factor-rotation invariance of the readout, \cref{ssec:readout}), \emph{every} latent-space $R^2$
--- filtered, one-step, and the forecast $R^2_{\mathrm{fc}}(k)$ that defines $H$ --- is scored
through a single best-fit affine alignment between the inferred and true latents (least squares;
this absorbs the rotation, scaling, reflection, and offset, and is fit once per run, not re-fit per
prediction). $H$ is the longest lead \emph{time} over which the dynamics-only $k$-step prediction
retains at least half of the model's own filtering accuracy (the largest $k$ with
$R^2_{\mathrm{fc}}(k)\ge 0.5\,R^2_{\mathrm{filter}}$, times the bin width). $H$ is a general
predictability time; for this limit-cycle benchmark it is equivalently read in periods
($1$ period $\approx0.21$\,s). Normalizing the threshold to the achievable accuracy keeps $H$
meaningful when that accuracy is itself low, and, unlike a $k$-averaged skill score, $H$ does not
shrink as the evaluation length grows. We also report the steady per-bin compute time. We report mean $\pm$ s.e.\ over
paired seeds, and sweep the smoothing $\tau\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ across all five SNR.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\emph{The projection encoder is a robust, readout-agnostic win; the decoupled online readout
recovers the latent online and its value is greatest at low SNR --- where readout estimation is
hard --- while at high SNR a frozen principal-component readout is already near-oracle; and once
the online readout converges, freezing the refresh recovers the forecast accuracy that continued
rewriting erodes.} \Cref{tab:r2} (filtered-latent $R^2$) and \cref{tab:onestep} (one-step
prediction $R^2$), with \cref{fig:summary,fig:curves}, establish the SNR dependence;
\cref{fig:readout} isolates the refresh schedule, \cref{fig:tau} the smoothing, and
\cref{fig:timing} the real-time cost.

\textbf{(1) The projection input is a clear, robust win at fixed readout, and it pays most where
the latent is hardest to read.} With the readout fixed (oracle $\vC$), projection$+$oracle beats
spike$+$oracle at every SNR in filtered-latent $R^2$ (\cref{tab:r2}), the margin peaking at low SNR
($0.74$ vs.\ $0.52$ at $0$\,dB; $+0.22$). On one-step prediction the gain is largest at mid SNR
($0.84$ vs.\ $0.59$ at $3$\,dB; \cref{tab:onestep}); at $-3$\,dB one-step prediction is near the
floor for every method ($\approx0.1$), the dynamics carrying little information at that noise
level. Feeding the recognition network the readout's own approximate inverse spares it a
high-dimensional, near-silent inversion exactly when each spike is most precious.
\textbf{(2) The online readout recovers the latent online, and its value is greatest at low SNR.}
At $0$\,dB the decoupled online readout reaches $0.72$ --- within $0.02$ of the projection$+$oracle
ceiling ($0.74$) and far above both the raw-spike bound ($0.52$) and the simple frozen-PCA baseline
($0.55$; \cref{tab:r2}, \cref{fig:summary}) --- recovering a latent that the alternatives cannot.
At high SNR the regime is easy: a frozen batch PCA fit on a short window is already within $0.04$ of
the oracle ($0.92$ vs.\ $0.96$ at $8$\,dB), so the online machinery has little to add there, and
indeed its estimate is still converging at $1000$\,s (\cref{fig:curves}).
\textbf{(3) The best readout schedule depends on the regime: lock once converged at high SNR, keep
adapting at low SNR.} At high SNR the Procrustes anchor keeps the latent frame essentially fixed ---
the tracked subspace drifts only $0.2^\circ$ over the whole stream --- so frame rotation is
\emph{not} the issue; rather, each rewrite of $\vC$ keeps nudging the target the dynamics learner
fits. \Cref{fig:readout} sweeps the four schedules across SNR. At high SNR, locking the readout
once it converges (slow/locked) more than doubles the free-run forecast horizon of the fast,
continuously-rewriting readout --- from $0.41$\,s to $0.92$\,s ($1.9\!\to\!4.4$ periods), matching
the oracle ($>1.0$\,s, censored at the evaluation window) and a good fixed PCA ($0.95$\,s), and a
fixed readout is near-oracle on one-step prediction ($0.97$); freezing costs nothing in filtering.
At low SNR the picture inverts: the subspace is genuinely moving ($14^\circ$ drift at $0$\,dB),
free-run forecasting is infeasible for every schedule (horizon $\approx0$; \cref{fig:kstep}), and
continued adaptation now \emph{helps} --- at $-3$\,dB the adaptive readouts lead on one-step
prediction ($0.30$ online, $0.28$ slow/locked) while a frozen PCA collapses ($-0.02$) and even the
oracle readout reaches only $0.10$. The recipe follows the regime: freeze for a stationary,
high-SNR recording; keep adapting when SNR is low or the readout drifts.
\textbf{(4) Smoothing helps the noisy regime and rate fidelity.} The EMA denoises the readout
feature, and the benefit is largest at low SNR (\cref{fig:tau}): rate correlation and
filtered-latent $R^2$ rise with $\tau$ up to $\tau\!\approx\!4$--$8$ in the noisy conditions,
while over-smoothing ($\tau\gtrsim16$) hurts through lag, mildly at high SNR.
\textbf{(5) Real time.} Per-bin online cost is flat at a median of $\sim2.8$\,ms from $n{=}15$ to
$250$ on a single CPU core (\cref{fig:timing}), with a tight bulk (interquartile range
$<0.3$\,ms; p95 $<3.1$\,ms, p99 $<3.3$\,ms on uncontended conditions), comfortably inside the
$5$\,ms bin budget, and zero divergences over the $1000$\,s pass. The rare millisecond-scale
excursions above budget are intermittent host scheduling jitter on the shared cloud VM ---
time-correlated and untied to $n$ or to the algorithm (they move across conditions when the run is
repeated, and vanish on an isolated VM), not a property of \svjf. These are reference numbers for
an unoptimized, single-threaded PyTorch implementation on one core of a modest cloud VM
(e2-standard-4); the cost is flat in $n$ because it is dominated by fixed per-bin work (the
recognition network and the square-root-RLS update) rather than the $O(nm)$ projection and readout,
so it is readily accelerated by vectorization, a GPU, or a compiled inference path. (We pin a
single thread deliberately: for these small per-bin matrices, multi-threaded BLAS adds fork-join
overhead that inflates the tail without lowering the median.)

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
With an unknown observation model, real-time \svjf\ is feasible at biological rates and real-time
cost: a cheap small-window readout warm-start, the projection encoder, and the two-timescale online
readout recover the latent dynamics online, and the projection encoder is a robust improvement over
raw-spike recognition even when the readout is known. \emph{The gains concentrate where they
matter --- the low-SNR regime of sparse, few-neuron recordings; at high SNR the problem is easy
enough that a frozen principal-component readout is already near-oracle, so the online machinery
earns its keep precisely where readout estimation is hard.} The one design tension is the readout
schedule. Continued refresh keeps the latent frame essentially fixed (the tracked subspace drifts
under a degree over the stream), but each rewrite still nudges the target the recursive dynamics
learner is chasing, which caps one-step prediction and forecast horizon at high SNR even when the
filtered-latent estimate is already accurate. Freezing or annealing the refresh once the subspace
has converged removes this and recovers forecast accuracy at no cost to filtering --- the practical
recipe for a stationary recording. The open question is the non-stationary case: one then wants the
readout to keep tracking, so a principled trigger for when to re-enable (or how to anneal) the
refresh --- for instance a drift- or likelihood-based change detector --- is the natural next step.

\paragraph{Relation to XFADS.} The projection encoder shares with XFADS
\autocite{Dowling2024b} the strategy of summarizing each high-dimensional observation into a
low-dimensional latent-space quantity before inference, rather than feeding the recognition
machinery the raw spikes. The constructions are opposite, however: XFADS \emph{learns} an
amortized network that emits the natural parameters of a per-timestep Gaussian pseudo-likelihood
(an information vector and a low-rank precision), trained end-to-end through the ELBO and
independent of the readout; we instead use a \emph{fixed} least-squares projection
$\vpi_t=\vC^{+}(\tilde g(\vy_t)-\vb)$ built explicitly from a separately, online-estimated loading
$\vC$. We thus replace XFADS's learned amortization of the observation-to-latent map with an
analytic, link-matched projection that exploits the known generative structure.

These results are, to our knowledge, the first demonstration of real-time VJF with an unknown
readout (\svjf); the scope is, however, a single synthetic benchmark (one limit-cycle trajectory class,
log-linear Poisson observations, no real data). Validating on recorded population activity and on
richer dynamics is the natural next step.

\section{Supplementary: flow-learner comparison}\label{app:flow}
\Cref{fig:flow} isolates the flow learner of \cref{ssec:srrls}. We hold the readout fixed at the
oracle (projection encoder, frozen $\vC,\vb$) so the only thing that varies is how the RBF velocity
weights $\vW$ are learned, and train the recognition by the same optimizer across the recursive
arms. With a clean readout the flow learner is not a large accuracy
differentiator --- square-root RLS, Adam, and SGD all reach one-step $R^2$ in the $0.88$--$0.97$
range (filtered $R^2$ is essentially tied). Square-root RLS converges fastest in the early stream;
SGD starts slower but catches up to a marginally higher steady state; Adam plateaus a little lower.
The one decisive effect is stability: plain RLS diverges within a few thousand steps (the precision
wind-up of \cref{ssec:srrls}). In a streaming setting --- accurate dynamics \emph{quickly} and
\emph{without} blowing up --- square-root RLS is the pragmatic choice; gradient flow learning is a
viable alternative.
